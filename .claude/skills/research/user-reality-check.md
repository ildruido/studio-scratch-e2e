# Branch: user reality check

Does the target group exist, does it have the problem, and how does it cope today? Run it before anyone describes the target users in detail or builds for them. It adds to the research skill's steps and removes none.

**Default tier: standard.** The check decides whether the product is for people who exist and have this problem, so a wrong answer is expensive. Go deep if the whole bet rests on one segment that the evidence leaves unclear.

- **Scope is a product decision.** If the launch markets (countries, languages) aren't settled in the app's documents, ask the App Creator before pre-registering: one question, with your recommended answer.
- **Time box:** about one working session by default. Record it in the protocol.

## Standard hypotheses

Pre-register all six, worded for this product's target users and the job the product does for them. U1 to U4 are key; U5 and U6 are not. Set the numbers in each kill criterion in the protocol, before searching. You may add hypotheses; don't drop these.

| Id | Hypothesis | Null or strongest alternative | Example kill criterion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U1 Exists | The target group exists in meaningful numbers in the launch markets. | It is too small or too scattered to build for. | Credible sources put the size below the threshold set in the protocol. No usable size source is Insufficient evidence, not a trigger. |
| U2 Does the job | Within that group, members themselves do the job the product does, rather than intermediaries (agencies, managers, outside services). | Someone else does it, or nobody does. | Most evidence shows the job is handed to others or skipped. |
| U3 Job hurts | The job costs them meaningful time, money or results, and they say so without being prompted. | It is a minor annoyance next to their real problems. | The pain shows up mainly when prompted or in vendor material, or it ranks low among their problems. |
| U4 Workarounds fall short | They use tools or people today, and the same shortcomings come up again and again. | What they use today (free tools, friends, freelancers) is good enough. | Satisfaction with current workarounds outweighs complaints in independent sources. |
| U5 Angle matters | The product's distinctive angle (from the app's principles) matters to them. | They want something generic, or don't care. | Evidence shows a clear preference against the angle, or indifference. |
| U6 Would pay (signal only) | They already pay for comparable help. | They don't pay for this kind of help. | Independent evidence that they do not pay for comparable help today. Desk research rarely settles this either way: expect Insufficient evidence and list it for primary research. |

Also ask, as a neutral question: **what are this group's biggest problems, in their own words?** If the job the product does is not near the top, that is a finding, not noise.

## Source mix

- U1 needs at least one grade A source (official or industry statistics with a method, platform data reports, academic work). A vendor's size estimate is a lead, not evidence.
- U2 to U5 need first-hand voices from at least three independent communities (different platforms, niches or countries).
- Include those who quit or failed: people or groups who stopped doing the job, tools people abandoned and why.
- Vendor material never carries a hypothesis.

## Look for variation

As you log evidence, note the attributes that vary across it: role in the group, stage, how often they do the job, budget, skills, tools, who helps them, niche, country. Segments split where the evidence splits, not where imagination does.

## Type-specific checks (pre-register in the protocol)

- The size threshold for U1 and the numbers in each kill criterion.
- The minimum of three independent communities for U2 to U5, and how independence is judged.
- The planned search for people who quit or failed.

## Extra outputs in the report

After Findings, add:

- **Evidence-backed traits:** a table of trait, the variation seen, evidence ids and confidence. Whoever later describes the target users builds on these and marks anything else as an assumption.
- **Segment candidates:** two to four clusters the evidence suggests, with what sets each apart and how strong the evidence for it is.
- **Look-alikes:** people who look like targets but aren't, with the evidence.
- **Open questions for interviews:** what only talking to real people can settle.

A verdict of Reshape proposes the change (a different target group, a different job, a narrower segment). The App Creator decides it, and the decision is recorded, before work that depends on the target users goes on.
