# Branch: product foundation

Builds on: the Discovery profile's Starting idea. Read it as a starting point, not a fixed plan: the interview may move away from it, and the starting idea file itself is never edited.

## Outline

- **Problem:** whose problem, what it costs them today, and how they cope now.
- **Target market:** who it is for and who it is not for; where it launches first.
- **Vision:** what is different for these users once the product works, in two or three sentences.
- **Value proposition:** why they would pick this over what they do today. One sentence, then the reasons.
- **Key concepts:** the few named ideas the product is built around, each in one line.
- **Principles:** the rules every later decision must respect. Three to five, each one something a real alternative would break.
- **Assumptions** and **Open questions.**

## Rules this document adds

- Question the value proposition hardest: "why would they switch from what they do today?" is asked before anything about features.
- A principle that no sensible product would break ("easy to use") is not a principle: ask for the trade-off it settles.
- No feature list here. Features belong in the MVP.
- Copy the principles into the Discovery profile's Principles row (`discovery-profile set`) in the same PR as the document, so they are approved together and every other skill reads the same words.
